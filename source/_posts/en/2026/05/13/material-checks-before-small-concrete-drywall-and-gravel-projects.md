# Material Checks Before Small Concrete, Drywall, and Gravel Projects

Some home project materials are annoying to under-order. A missing box of flooring is inconvenient, but a shortage of concrete, drywall sheets, or driveway gravel can stop the work completely. These materials are heavy, delivery-sensitive, and sometimes difficult to return.

Before buying, I like to run a quick quantity check in the same format the store uses: bags, sheets, cubic yards, or tons.

## Concrete Bags for Small Slabs

For a small concrete pad, the volume is:

```text
cubic feet = length x width x depth in feet
```

Depth is often measured in inches, so convert it first:

```text
depth in feet = depth in inches / 12
```

For a 10 ft by 8 ft slab at 4 inches deep:

```text
10 x 8 x (4 / 12) = 26.67 cubic feet
```

If an 80 lb bag yields about 0.6 cubic feet:

```text
26.67 / 0.6 = 44.45 bags
```

Round up to 45 bags.

That is a lot of bags for what sounds like a small slab, which is why this check is useful. For larger pours, ready-mix may be more practical.

## Concrete for Post Holes

Post holes are a different shape. They are cylinders, so the volume uses radius and depth:

```text
hole volume = pi x radius squared x depth
```

The diameter is often measured in inches. A 12 inch diameter hole has a 6 inch radius, or 0.5 feet. If the hole is 36 inches deep, the depth is 3 feet.

For four holes:

```text
pi x 0.5 x 0.5 x 3 x 4 = 9.42 cubic feet
```

At 0.6 cubic feet per bag:

```text
9.42 / 0.6 = 15.7 bags
```

Round up to 16 bags.

## Drywall Sheet Count

Drywall estimates start with covered area:

```text
covered area = wall area + ceiling area - openings
```

Then add waste and divide by sheet size:

```text
sheets = adjusted square feet / sheet square feet
```

A 4 ft by 8 ft sheet covers 32 square feet. A room with 740 covered square feet and 10% waste needs:

```text
740 x 1.10 = 814 adjusted square feet
814 / 32 = 25.4 sheets
```

Round up to 26 sheets.

## Gravel for Driveways

Driveway gravel is usually planned in cubic yards or tons:

```text
cubic yards = length x width x depth in feet / 27
tons = cubic yards x tons per cubic yard
```

For a 40 ft by 10 ft driveway at 4 inches deep:

```text
40 x 10 x (4 / 12) / 27 = 4.94 cubic yards
```

If the gravel density estimate is 1.4 tons per cubic yard:

```text
4.94 x 1.4 = 6.91 tons
```

## Final Notes

These estimates are not engineering plans. Local code, frost depth, soil conditions, compaction, and product labels still matter. But a quick quantity check catches obvious mistakes before you order heavy materials.
