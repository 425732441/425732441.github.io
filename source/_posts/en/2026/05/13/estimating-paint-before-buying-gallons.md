# Estimating Paint Before Buying Gallons

Paint is one of those projects that looks simple until you are standing in the aisle trying to decide whether one gallon is enough. The usual advice is to measure the room, check the label, and add a little extra, but that still leaves a few details open: openings, ceiling height, number of coats, and the actual coverage rate of the paint.

For small interior projects, I like to make the estimate in square feet first and only convert to gallons at the end. That makes the assumptions easier to see and easier to adjust.

## Start With Wall Area

For a simple rectangular room, the wall area is:

```text
wall area = 2 x (room width + room length) x wall height
```

For example, a 12 ft by 10 ft room with 8 ft walls gives:

```text
2 x (12 + 10) x 8 = 352 square feet
```

That is the gross wall area before subtracting anything.

## Subtract Openings

Doors, windows, built-ins, cabinets, or sections you will not paint should be subtracted. You do not need a perfect architectural takeoff for a normal repaint. A rough total is usually enough.

For the same room, if the doors and windows add up to about 40 square feet:

```text
352 - 40 = 312 paintable square feet
```

This is the number I want before thinking about gallons.

## Add Coats and Waste

The number of coats matters more than many people expect. A color change, patchy wall, or low-hide paint can double the actual paintable area.

```text
adjusted square feet = paintable square feet x coats x waste factor
```

If the room needs one coat and I add 10% waste:

```text
312 x 1.10 = 343.2 adjusted square feet
```

If it needs two coats:

```text
312 x 2 x 1.10 = 686.4 adjusted square feet
```

That difference is the reason a simple "one gallon covers a room" rule is risky.

## Convert Square Feet to Gallons

Paint labels often list coverage around 350 to 400 square feet per gallon, but the exact number depends on the product and surface. Rough texture, primer needs, or very dark colors can change the result.

```text
gallons = adjusted square feet / coverage per gallon
```

Using 350 square feet per gallon:

```text
343.2 / 350 = 0.98 gallons
```

In practice, that means buying 1 gallon for this example. If the result were 1.2 gallons, I would usually plan for 2 gallons unless I had a very specific way to use quarts.

## Final Check Before Buying

Before you buy paint, confirm:

- product coverage on the label
- whether primer is needed
- number of coats
- surface texture
- doors and windows you are subtracting
- whether you need touch-up paint later

The calculator gives a planning estimate, not a professional quote. But writing the formula down and checking each assumption makes the buying decision much less random.
